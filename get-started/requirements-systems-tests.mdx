Flow becomes useful once items are connected. This guide builds one short thread: a system, what it must achieve, and the evidence that shows how close it is. The same pattern works for any structure your team models, such as interfaces, parts, analyses, or risks.

You need permission to edit the current branch, and a data model with the categories you plan to use. Category and field names depend on your project. If the current branch is protected, [create a branch](/get-started/creating-a-branch) first.

## 1. Create a system and give it an owner

Use the create control in your current view and choose your project's systems category. Name the item, place it under its parent system, and describe its boundary: what is inside it and what it connects to.

Set the **Owner** to the engineer closest to that part of the design. They are best placed to keep it current.

## 2. Record what it must achieve

Add the item that states the intent. That is often a requirement, but it can be an interface definition, a design target, or a budget, depending on your model. Put measurable values in number fields as well as in the text, so Flow can compare them with results later.

For example, a vehicle team records an **Aerodynamic Drag Coefficient** target of 0.28 against its **Body & Aerodynamics** system.

## 3. Connect it

Connect the new item to the system that delivers it, and to anything it comes from, such as a higher-level target. Those links answer two questions every engineer asks: where is this delivered, and why does it exist?

Check each relationship from both sides. A link's type and direction carry its meaning; two items that only sit near each other in a table are not connected. If the relationship you need is missing, ask your Admin to check the data model.

## 4. Record the evidence

Add how you will know the intent is met, such as a test case or an analysis, and connect it. Record each result as its own item with the measured value, date, and conditions, and attach the report.

Keep results separate rather than overwriting one value. In the vehicle example, three prototype builds measured 0.296, 0.287, and 0.279; only the last meets the target, and the history shows the trend toward it.

## Check the thread

Start at the system and follow it to what it must achieve, who owns it, and the latest evidence. Then start from a result and follow it back up.

If another engineer can make that trip without asking you anything, the thread is complete. [The systems graph](/core-concepts/the-systems-graph) explains why it matters; [Getting data into Flow](/get-started/getting-data-into-flow) shows how to bring in the records you already have.
