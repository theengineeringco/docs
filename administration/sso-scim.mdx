---
title: "SSO and SCIM"
description: "Sign people in through your identity provider and provision their accounts and groups automatically."
---

SSO signs people in through your identity provider. SCIM lets that provider create and deactivate users and manage groups in Flow.

Both live in **Settings → SSO and Provisioning**, which has three tabs: **SSO Setup**, **SSO Roles**, and **SCIM Provisioning**. Configuring SSO and SCIM needs an Admin with access to all projects. Self-serve **SSO Setup** appears only where your deployment enables it; otherwise, contact Flow. You also need access to your identity provider and your DNS.

## Set up SSO

1. In **SSO Setup**, copy the redirect URI and register it in your provider's OIDC application.
2. Enter a **Button label**, the **OIDC issuer URL**, **Client ID**, **Client secret**, and **Allowed email domains**. Use the provider's issuer value, not its authorization or token endpoint.
3. Choose whether to **Require SSO (hide password sign-in for these domains)** and whether to **Show only the SSO button (skip email routing)**. Select **Create connection**.
4. Publish each TXT record Flow shows at your DNS provider, then select **Check DNS verification**. DNS can take a few minutes to propagate.
5. When the domains verify, select **Activate SSO**. Sign in through the new button to confirm it works.

**Disable SSO** takes the button down without losing verification; **Re-enable SSO** puts it back. Changing the allowed domains on a live connection returns it to draft until you verify DNS and activate again. Other edits keep it live.

## Map identity-provider roles

On **SSO Roles**, map a role from your provider, or every user from a provider, to a [user group](/administration/user-groups). At each sign-in, Flow adds the user to groups whose mapping they match and removes them from the rest. If sign-ins do not land in the groups you expect, expand **What your IdP is sending** to see the claims Flow received.

Under **Automatic user provisioning**, choose the role and projects a person gets the first time they sign in through a provider. When it is off, people need an invitation first. Email and password sign-ins always need an invitation.

## Set up SCIM

1. On **SCIM Provisioning**, copy the **SCIM URL** into your provider.
2. Set **Role for provisioned users** and **Projects for provisioned users**. Admin is not available as a provisioned role.
3. Optionally, choose **Groups that grant Member**: Viewers in these SCIM groups become Members.
4. Select **Generate Token**, name it, and copy the token. Flow shows it only once. Enter it in your provider.
5. Provision a test user and group. Check the user's role and access, and that the group shows the **SCIM** tag in **Settings → User Groups**.

Treat a SCIM token as an administrative credential. To stop a connection, revoke its token; set up a replacement first if provisioning must continue.

## What SCIM changes

- Removing or deactivating a user in your provider deactivates them in Flow. They keep their record, role, and project access, and reactivating them restores access.
- Deleting a group in your provider deletes it in Flow.
- SCIM groups have read-only membership in Flow and cannot also use an SSO role mapping.
- Passwords stay in your identity provider; SCIM does not change them.
